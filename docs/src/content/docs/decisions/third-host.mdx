The question, asked on 2026-10-06, was what to do with the Windows 11 machine that ran Plex and seven Docker media apps (an i7-8700K with 12 threads, 32 GB of RAM, an ASRock Z370 board, a 250 GB NVMe, five SATA disks in an Intel RST RAID 0 and six USB portables). Four explorer agents and one synthesizer researched it, the lead re-verified the load-bearing claims, and the operator made the calls the same day. One storage call was revised on 2026-10-07 after a disk failed its self-test.

Claims carry the labels the research used. "Fetched" means the page was read, "snippet" means only a search summary was seen, "repo" names a file in this repository, and UNVERIFIED means nothing was checked. Where an explorer's reasoning rather than a source carries a point, the sentence says so.

The steps that carry this out are in [The media host](/hosts/third-host/). This page is the record of what was decided and why.

## What was decided

- The machine becomes the media host: a standalone Proxmox VE 9.2 install on the NVMe, single-disk ZFS root, with the e1000e offload fix applied up front.
- No new disks. The operator decided at 16:34 on 2026-10-06 that nothing will be bought and that wiping the media and starting fresh is acceptable.
- The media library is a ZFS pool on the host, built from the RAID 0's members, which measured as five WD Blue 4 TB CMR disks at about 64,670 power-on hours each. The design was five-wide RAIDZ2 at 12 TB usable. Revised 2026-10-07: one member failed its long self-test three minutes in, and the operator chose RAIDZ2 on the four survivors (8 TB usable, any two failures) over the RAIDZ1 fallback and over buying a disk. The pool can grow to five-wide in place with `zpool attach` if a disk is ever added.
- One dataset holds the library and the downloads in the TRaSH layout from day one (`usenet/{incomplete,complete/{tv,movies}}` and `media/{tv,movies}`), with `recordsize=1M`, `compression=lz4`, `atime=off`, ashift 12, the ARC capped at 8 to 12 GiB, a monthly scrub and `smartd` mail on every disk. It is exported once over NFS and bind-mounted into the Plex LXC.
- Every media file is rebuilt. Sonarr and Radarr re-fetch what their databases list as wanted, within the pool's ceiling. The Sonarr, Radarr and SABnzbd configurations are carried over through each app's own UI backup. Plex, Tautulli and Maintainerr start fresh.
- Plex runs in an unprivileged Ubuntu 24.04 LXC on the host, from the official deb, with the UHD 630 render node handed in through `dev0` and the library bind-mounted.
- One Talos worker VM, `k8s-worker-4`, 6 vCPU and 20 GB of RAM, on the NVMe's `local-zfs` with a 100 GB system disk, joined through the repo's `[[nodes]]` pattern. Its address is outside the ranges the other nodes use and inside `node_cidr`.
- The media apps go straight into the cluster, in a `restricted` `media` namespace over NFS from the pool, with Sonarr as the pilot. That is the media stack decision, recorded on its own page.
- The USB portables are never pool members. After the wipe, the clean ones become the backup layer (a `vzdump` target for the Plex LXC and the worker VM, rotated with one kept offline, and one holding the UI backup zips), one is the spare, and the two with pending sectors are retired.

## Why

### Proxmox on the host, not a NAS VM or a bigger cluster

A fourth control plane adds no fault tolerance, because etcd wants an odd member count (fetched: [etcd FAQ](https://etcd.io/docs/v3.6/faq/)). The host has 12 threads and 32 GB; the existing workers are 6 vCPU and 30 GB (repo: the Proxmox host guide), and 30 GB does not fit beside Plex, so the worker is 20 GB. The board tops out at 64 GB even though the CPU supports 128 GB (fetched: [ASRock manual](https://download.asrock.com/Manual/Z370%20Killer%20SLIac.pdf)), so a RAM upgrade is the one thing that would let the worker match the 30 GB pattern. The installer's single-disk `zfs (RAID0)` keeps the `local-zfs` storage name that the host guide's `mkvm` expects, and the installer sets the ARC limit to 10% of RAM (fetched: [Proxmox sysadmin chapter](https://pve.proxmox.com/pve-docs/chapter-sysadmin.html)). The board's NIC is an Intel I219V on `e1000e` despite the "Killer" name, and it has the TSO/GSO hang that hits VM bridges; Proxmox staff recommended turning the offloads off (fetched: [forum thread 106001](https://forum.proxmox.com/threads/intel-nic-e1000e-hardware-unit-hang.106001/)). The thread covers kernels 6.5 and 6.8, nothing was found for 7.0, so the fix is applied as cheap insurance.

### No new disks, and a smaller library

The arithmetic, measured on 2026-10-06 with `Get-Volume`: 18.6 TB used on the RAID 0, 21.8 TB on five USB disks, up to 5 TB more on a sixth that Windows no longer mounted, about 41 TB in all. No layout of the disks owned holds 41 TB with redundancy. Redundancy means new disks, and at ZFS's 80% fill guidance (snippet: [OpenZFS discussion](https://github.com/openzfs/zfs/discussions/13511)) a pool for that library needs 52 to 58 TB usable, five or six 24 TB disks at $3.2k to $5.2k by late-2026 search prices plus an HBA for the copy-in. The operator chose a smaller redundant library over the purchase. The library ceiling is the pool's usable size, and the quality profiles chosen for the media stack set the per-title size. That is a change in what the library is, not only where it lives, and it was the operator's choice at 16:34.

### RAIDZ2 on the RAID 0's members

The members measured on 2026-10-06 at 17:34 with smartmontools 7.5 on Windows: five WD Blue WD40EZRZ, 3.5 inch, CMR per smartmontools' drive database, all at about 64,670 hours, which is 7.4 years of continuous spinning. Every one passed SMART, and one had 1 pending and 14 offline-uncorrectable sectors. RAIDZ1 would give 16 TB and tolerate one failure. With every member past seven years and one already flagged, a second failure during a resilver is the scenario to design against, so double parity won the 4 TB it cost. There is no spare of that size, so a failed member leaves the pool degraded until a replacement is bought, and RAIDZ2 keeps one parity in hand through that. The flagged member then failed the long test on 2026-10-07 (read failure, 14 offline-uncorrectable at 64,699 hours), which is why the pool is four-wide. RAIDZ expansion is `zpool attach` of one disk to a RAIDZ vdev on OpenZFS 2.3 and later; blocks written before the expansion keep the old data-to-parity ratio until rewritten (fetched: [zpool-attach](https://openzfs.github.io/openzfs-docs/man/master/8/zpool-attach.8.html)). Proxmox 9.2 ships ZFS 2.4 (fetched: [Proxmox roadmap](https://pve.proxmox.com/wiki/Roadmap)).

The Windows RAID could not be converted in place. Intel's migration table lists RAID 0 to RAID 5 with added disks and not RAID 0 to RAID 1, and a migration rewrites every member in place, the wrong risk with a suspect disk (fetched: [Intel RST migration article](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/support/articles/000005997/technologies.html), table detail from a search summary). Linux reads Intel RST through mdadm only with the BIOS in RAID mode, or in AHCI mode with `IMSM_NO_PLATFORM=1` (fetched: [mdadm manual](https://man.archlinux.org/man/mdadm.8)). With the fresh start, none of that matters: the installer leaves the members alone and `wipefs` clears them.

### One filesystem for library and downloads

Hardlinks and atomic moves need one real filesystem (fetched: [TRaSH file and folder structure](https://trash-guides.info/File-and-Folder-Structure/)). Child ZFS datasets are separate filesystems to a hardlink (snippet only, general ZFS behaviour), so `media/` and `usenet/` are plain directories in one dataset. Downloads live on the pool with the library because the USB disks are too warm (43 to 47 C against 28 to 30 C for the SATA members) and shingled for the incomplete-download churn, and a scratch disk off the pool would break hardlinks. The pool settings come from the OpenZFS tuning guide (fetched: [workload tuning](https://openzfs.github.io/openzfs-docs/Performance%20and%20Tuning/Workload%20Tuning.html)); ashift cannot change later (fetched: [ZFS on Linux](https://pve.proxmox.com/wiki/ZFS_on_Linux)). Proxmox's ARC guideline of 2 GiB plus 1 GiB per TiB cannot be met on 32 GB beside VMs, so the 8 to 12 GiB cap is the lead's judgment.

### The USB portables are not pool members

TrueNAS's hardware guide says to avoid USB-connected disks for primary storage and that USB media may report serial numbers inaccurately (fetched: [TrueNAS hardware guide](https://www.truenas.com/docs/scale/gettingstarted/tnhardwareguide/)). Its forum resource adds that SMART is often not passed through, connectors unlatch, cooling fails under scrub and resilver, and bridge firmware is cheap (fetched: [why to avoid USB drives](https://forums.truenas.com/t/1499)). The reliability counters read on Windows came back empty, which is that SMART gap. The WD 5 TB portables are 2.5-inch drives on a USB-native board that cannot be shucked (snippet: retailer reviews), and 5 TB in 2.5 inches is almost certainly shingled, which ZFS resilvers cannot tolerate (snippet: [ServeTheHome SMR test](https://www.servethehome.com/wd-red-smr-vs-cmr-tested-avoid-red-smr/), 17 hours CMR against 230 hours SMR). The "Seagate Expansion" turned out to be a 2.5-inch laptop drive with 16 pending sectors at 1,374 hours, not a shuckable desktop disk. Sequential backup writes are the one workload a shingled USB disk does well, and TrueNAS's guide allows USB for exactly that, which is why the clean ones became the backup layer.

### Rebuild the media, keep the app configurations

Sonarr and Radarr skip the scan of a series whose folder is missing while the root folder is empty, and purge that series' file records once the root folder has content (fetched: [DiskScanService.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sonarr/Sonarr/develop/src/NzbDrone.Core/MediaFiles/DiskScanService.cs)). So a restored database with re-pointed root folders rebuilds the library through the missing-episode and missing-movie searches, provided "Unmonitor Deleted Episodes" and "Unmonitor Deleted Movies" are off during that pass. The UI backups are the sanctioned restore path: Sonarr and Radarr's System, Backup zips (fetched: [Servarr FAQ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Servarr/Wiki/master/sonarr/faq.md)), SABnzbd's Config, General backup (fetched: [SABnzbd general settings](https://sabnzbd.org/wiki/configuration/4.5/general)). Bazarr's UI restore has failed repeatedly (fetched: [bazarr issue 2748](https://github.com/morpheus65535/bazarr/issues/2748)), and Seerr has no UI backup (fetched: [Seerr backups](https://docs.seerr.dev/using-seerr/backups/)), so those two are hand copies. Tautulli and Maintainerr key their records on Plex rating keys that a fresh library does not carry (fetched: [Tautulli FAQ](https://docs.tautulli.com/support/frequently-asked-questions)), so they start fresh. Plex's watch history is the one real loss; whether a re-added item with the same metadata match keeps its watched state is UNVERIFIED.

### Plex in an LXC

The host keeps `i915`, no vfio binding, no ROM file, no display loss. The `dev0` syntax is in the Proxmox manual (fetched: [pct.conf](https://pve.proxmox.com/pve-docs/pct.conf.5.html)) and two working write-ups cover it on PVE 8.3 to 9 (fetched: [forum thread 158325](https://forum.proxmox.com/threads/158325/); [binarypatrick.dev](https://binarypatrick.dev/posts/2026/plex-lxc-hardware-transcoding/)). Plex lists Ubuntu as a supported Linux and hardware transcoding needs Plex Pass (fetched: [Plex hardware-accelerated streaming](https://support.plex.tv/articles/115002178853)). The one trap is the gid on the `dev0` line, which is the render group inside the container, not on the host (fetched: binarypatrick.dev). All four explorers independently landed on this placement and the lead's re-verification agreed; agreement is evidence, not proof, which is why each piece cites its source.

### The media apps in the cluster, over NFS

The `restricted` profile allows only configMap, csi, downwardAPI, emptyDir, ephemeral, persistentVolumeClaim, projected and secret volumes (verified by the lead, fetched: [Pod Security Standards](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/security/pod-security-standards/)), so media reaches a pod through a PVC bound to a static NFS PV, never an inline `nfs` or `hostPath` volume. SQLite on NFS or SMB corrupts (fetched: Servarr FAQ; [Seerr Docker page](http://docs.seerr.dev/getting-started/docker/)), so config stays on `local-path`. The Talos NFS client ships in the kubelet image (verified by the lead). An NFS server in an LXC needs `nesting` and `nfs` features or a privileged container, which the pct docs call unsafe (fetched: [pct chapter](https://pve.proxmox.com/pve-docs/chapter-pct.html)), so the host exports the pool itself.

### The worker needs no repo change beyond its node entry

The etcd scrape lists controllers only, the control-plane taint removal needs `controller_count == nodes | length` and stays false, and spegel, Cilium BGP and the `local-path` user volume under `talos/all/` pick a new node up on their own (explorer-cluster, repo reads). The nvidia plugin's affinity skips a node without the extension label. The worker's MAC in `cluster.toml` must match the VM, because every node gets the `all/` patches including the single-link `bond0` and the kubelet `validSubnets` (repo: the Talos network link patch).

## What was rejected

- Keeping the NTFS volumes in place under `ntfs3` and mounting them at the Windows drive-letter paths. It was the baseline for a day, when the operator wanted to avoid a wipe, and it cost checksums, scrub, snapshots and the single-filesystem layout; whether `ntfs3` creates hardlinks is UNVERIFIED (fetched: [ntfs3 docs](https://docs.kernel.org/filesystems/ntfs3.html)). The fresh start removed it.
- Buying five or six 24 TB disks for a 72 to 96 TB RAIDZ2 pool, plus an HBA, so the whole 41 TB library survives. The operator chose a smaller library over about $3.2k to $5.2k.
- RAIDZ1 on the members: 16 TB usable, but one parity disk on seven-year-old members with one already flagged.
- A 20 TB stripe of the members: no redundancy, settled against on 2026-10-06 at 17:41.
- Mirrors, four-wide RAIDZ1 on 24 TB disks (a day or more without redundancy during a resilver), and dRAID2 (fixed stripe width wastes space and it does not grow; fetched: [dRAID howto](https://openzfs.github.io/openzfs-docs/Basic%20Concepts/Pool%20Structure/dRAID%20Howto.html)).
- The USB portables as pool members, or passed into a VM: no stable serial, UAS resets, SMR, and a disk mounted on the host must never also be passed to a VM (explorer-hardware, reasoning).
- Plex in a VM with the iGPU passed through: Code 43 reports on PVE 9 for Windows guests, a Coffee Lake ROM file and UEFI-only boot for legacy-igd, and the host loses its iGPU and console (fetched: [intel-igpu-passthru](https://github.com/LongQT-sea/intel-igpu-passthru); snippet: [forum thread 169951](https://forum.proxmox.com/threads/pve-9-0-4-igpu-passthrough-error-43.169951/)).
- Plex as a pod on the worker: needs the passthrough first, then the `siderolabs/i915` extension and the Intel device plugin or DRA driver, none of which the repo has, and the GPU plus `local-path` pin it to one node (fetched: [siderolabs/extensions](https://github.com/siderolabs/extensions); [Intel GPU plugin](https://github.com/intel/intel-device-plugins-for-kubernetes/blob/main/cmd/gpu_plugin/README.md)).
- An interim Compose VM for the seven apps, then a move into the cluster one at a time. Proxmox recommends Docker in a VM over an LXC (fetched: [forum thread 112004](https://forum.proxmox.com/threads/run-docker-inside-lxc.112004/)), and it was the first recommendation. The operator dropped it on 2026-10-06 along with the old compose file; the apps are redeployed from current guidance with only their configuration reimported.
- A NAS VM with the disks passed through: every byte to Plex would take two hops.
- Disks passed into the Talos VM: pins Plex and every media pod to one node, and Talos formats only ext4, xfs and btrfs (fetched: [Talos user volumes](https://docs.siderolabs.com/talos/v1.14/configure-your-talos-cluster/storage-and-disk-management/disk-management/user)).
- A fourth control plane (even etcd member count) or two small workers (double the kubelet, Cilium, spegel and node-exporter overhead for no isolation gain).
- A second SSD for VM disks. It was in the plan while the worker was to have a 200 GB disk beside a Compose VM; with the fresh start the worker stays at 100 GB on the NVMe and about 100 GB stays free for the LXC and headroom (arithmetic).
- Copying the RAID 0 and the USB disks off before the wipe, the `crossmnt` export with seven children, the seven `subPath` mounts, the `ntfs3` gating tests, and a `ddrescue` attempt on the unmounted USB disk. All fell with the fresh start.
- Plex through the Cloudflare tunnel. Cloudflare's terms restrict serving video through the CDN without its paid products and say nothing about Tunnel (fetched: Cloudflare's service-specific terms for application services, whose URL the scrub gate refuses for its length).

## What it rules out

- The library is capped by the pool: about 6.4 TB at 80% of 8 TB. Maintainerr and the quality profiles keep it inside that ceiling. A triage re-cut at that budget kept 217 titles at 5.5 TB.
- There is one copy of the media, under double parity, with no backup. RAIDZ2 does not protect against deletion, ransomware or fire; the library is re-downloadable, so the backup layer covers only the app configurations and the VM disks.
- A failed pool member leaves the pool degraded until a disk is bought. Nothing on hand matches the size.
- Plex is outside Flux and Renovate. It lives in the LXC and the host runbook, and a Talos node reboot does not touch it.
- Every media pod and Plex depend on the host's NFS export. An NFS outage stalls every media pod, and each app's config PVC pins it to one node.
- Downloads and library must stay in the one dataset. A second filesystem for either breaks hardlinks and the TRaSH layout.
- The pool's ashift and the `dev0` gid are set once; the ashift cannot change later.
- `zpool upgrade` on the 9.2 host makes the pool unreadable by an older rescue stick, so the rescue stick is 9.2.
- The USB disks cannot become ZFS members later without reopening the SMR and serial findings above.
- The worker is 20 GB, not the 30 GB the other workers have, until the host's RAM is upgraded to the board's 64 GB maximum.
- A RAM upgrade, a discrete GPU, or a decision that GitOps parity for Plex is worth a node-pinned single-GPU pod are the things that would reopen the Plex placement.
